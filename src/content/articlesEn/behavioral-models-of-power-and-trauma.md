## Methodology

This text is not clinical diagnosis and not moral judgment. It is a structural-psychological interpretation of publicly observable behavior of two political figures. All psychodynamic conclusions are hypothetical and based on open sources.

### Principles of analysis

- Only public, documented acts of behavior are compared.
- Psychological constructs are used as analytical language, not as clinical diagnoses.
- Each hypothesis is accompanied by reference to the factual basis and limitations.
- The goal is to identify structural parallels, not to prove identity.

## 1. Public behavioral models: what is visible from outside

### 1.1. Alexander Lukashenko – the model of "vertical and personalization"

Over more than 30 years in power, a model has been formed where the state and the regime are an extension of the will of one person. Political scientist Alexander Feduta back in 1998 described this rule as a "regime of personal power," where the key instrument is the personalization of politics and a vertical of subordination.

**Key traits:**

- **Personalization of conflict.** Criticism is addressed to the person, not the argument; the response is directed at a specific individual.
- **Binary thinking.** Rigid division into "us" and "enemies," absence of space for a neutral position.
- **Rudeness as an instrument of power.** Public insults of opponents, journalists, and officials serve as a demonstration of untouchable status.
- **Control of information.** Ideological vertical, pressure on independent media, attempts to regulate the agenda through administrative resources.
- **Family as a political structure.** Sons are involved in managerial and representative functions; the youngest son Nikolai is the public companion of his father, creating an image of dynasticism.

Psychiatric assessments that appeared in 2020 described "mosaic psychopathy" in Lukashenko, but these assessments are distant and debatable. For analysis, the behavioral pattern is more important: the inability to tolerate criticism and the tendency toward personalized aggression as a reaction to a threat to status.

### 1.2. Siarhei Tsikhanouski – the model of "reactive subjectivity"

After his release in June 2025, a model of behavior is demonstrated that sharply contrasts with expectations of the "hero of 2020." Over 15 months, a series of public incidents have accumulated.

**July 2025.** Volha Tokarchuk publishes a critical video. According to her, Tsikhanouski asked her not to mention him on her channel. Tokarchuk accuses him of having "lowered himself to the level of people" and of not accepting feedback.

**September 24, 2025.** New York incident. During a joint stream, Siarhei Tsikhanouski publicly demands that Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya cancel a CNN interview. The interview is canceled. On social media, this is perceived as public humiliation of his wife.

**October 2025.** Tsikhanouskaya about her husband: "Siarhei needs to choose his path: either you are in media and say whatever you want, or you are in politics."

**November 29-30, 2025.** Conflict with Siarhei Bespalov. The former press secretary accuses Tsikhanouski of rudeness, blackmail, unwillingness to listen to his circle. Bespalov directly calls him "Lukashenko 2.0."

**December 4, 2025.** Meduza summarizes the conflict: Tsikhanouski fights his wife's advisors instead of fighting Lukashenko.

**August 2026.** Escalation on social media. Tsikhanouski activates on Facebook, enters into altercations with journalists and commentators, uses insulting language ("clowns," "bark and stink"). In parallel, he voices the idea of appealing to Western donors with a proposal to "take into account the quality" of media in funding.

**Key traits of public behavior (post-2025):**

- **Impulsive aggression.** Public insults, emotional outbursts, inability to accept criticism.
- **Conflict with the inner circle.** Attack on the wife's office, former allies, media instead of consolidating the opposition.
- **Attempts to control the media field.** The idea of pressuring donors as a way to influence editorial policy.
- **Personalization of politics.** Focus on one's own role ("I suffered," "they profited from me").
- **Undefined status.** Constant change of roles: from hero-symbol to blogger-critic.

## 2. Psychodynamics: hypotheses and their limits

**Hypothesis.** The common trait of both figures is narcissistic vulnerability compensated by aggression. In Lukashenko, this is reinforced by 30 years of power and institutional impunity; in Tsikhanouski, by prison trauma and a sharp status rupture.

### 2.1. Narcissistic vulnerability and two interpretations

A behavioral analyst, commenting on Lukashenko's speeches in 2021, noted manifestations of fear and powerlessness masked by toughness. This corresponds to the classic model of the authoritarian personality: external control compensates for internal instability.

For Tsikhanouski, the status of "hero" was assigned to his wife and the office, while he perceives himself as the source of sacrifice. Aggression is directed not at the regime (which is physically inaccessible) but at the immediate circle. This is classic displaced aggression: the impossibility of attacking the real tyrant is transferred to accessible targets.

**Counter-hypothesis: conscious strategy of the "radical hero."** The behavior can also be read as a conscious strategy of conquering media space: creating an autonomous project, returning to the role of "main fighter," provocation as a resource for reach. If this hypothesis is correct, the behavior is not an impulse of trauma but an instrument. Both versions can be true simultaneously: trauma creates impulsivity, but the choice of channels and targets can be conscious.

### 2.2. The mechanism of the "paranoid narrative of siege"

A structurally important bridge between the two figures is the besieged fortress mechanism. Lukashenko: "they want to destroy us, we are a besieged fortress." Tsikhanouski: "they profited from me, they betrayed me, everyone around is clowns." In both cases, the world is divided into those who recognize the leader's sacrifice and those who dispute it; the latter become legitimate targets.

**Instruments of siege:**

- **Lukashenko:** KGB, Investigative Committee, security forces, administrative resources, legislative bans. The narrative serves to legitimize repression.
- **Tsikhanouski:** Reputational losses, harassment on social media, appeals to donors. The narrative serves to restore the status of victim.

The structure is the same: defining the enemy, demonizing them, attributing collective intent. The difference is in the scale of available instruments.

### 2.3. Trauma: explanation vs justification

Post-penitentiary syndrome - disruption of adaptation, hypervigilance, reduced impulse control - can explain part of the behavior. But it is critically important to distinguish between explanation (why?) and justification (is it permissible?).

An important clarification: the trauma hypothesis should not displace the alternative of a premorbid profile. Tsikhanouski demonstrated impulsivity and a provocative style even before 2020. Prison may have amplified traits rather than created them. Trauma is more of an amplifier than a root cause.

**Criterion of distinction:**

- Explanation answers the question "why?" and helps predict behavior.
- Justification answers the question "is it permissible?" and closes the discussion about consequences.
- Analytics must stop at the first.

**Limitation.** We do not have access to clinical data for either subject. All psychodynamic constructs are working metaphors that do not claim medical precision.

## 3. Comparative matrix of patterns

| Parameter | Lukashenko | Tsikhanouski |
|---|---|---|
| Type of aggression | Systemic, repressive | Reactive, impulsive |
| Criticism | Personal insult and threat to the regime | Betrayal and "working for the enemy" |
| Communication | Rudeness as demonstration of status | Rudeness as impulse and attempt to regain attention |
| Media | Ideological vertical, administrative control | Pressure on donors, harassment of journalists on social media |
| Family | Patriarchal vertical, dynasticism | Arena of conflict for redefining roles |
| Thinking | "Us - them," "patriots - traitors" | "Who is with me - who is against me," "clowns" |
| Narrative | "Besieged fortress," external threat | "They profited from me," internal betrayal |
| Resources | Monopoly on violence, 30 years of power | No political base, only social media and status |
| Trauma | Absent as a factor | Post-penitentiary syndrome as a possible amplifier |

**Interpretation.** The similarities are behavioral and structural; the differences are institutional and resource-based. Tsikhanouski demonstrates the pretensions of an authoritarian communication style without the authoritarian system in which they could be realized. This makes his behavior less dangerous in an institutional sense but more destructive in a symbolic one.

## 4. Impact on Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya and systemic risks

Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya found herself in a double bind: preserving the family after five years of separation and simultaneously preserving the trust of the team, donors, and international partners.

Her public statements demonstrate a strategy of neutralization: "Siarhei and I are not competitors," "the story about canceling the CNN interview was blown out of proportion." This is the classic caretaker position: she takes responsibility for the emotional stability of the family upon herself, minimizing damage to the project.

### 4.1. Gender irony: mirror of patriarchy

The Belarusian opposition has for decades criticized Lukashenko for patriarchalism. The symbol of the alternative became a female leader. The New York episode (canceling the CNN interview) creates structural irony: the husband publicly cancels his wife's working interview and commands her in front of an audience. This is not just a family conflict but a mirror reflection of what the democratic movement stands against.

### 4.2. Institutional immunity and its absence

In normal political structures, buffers exist between the leader's psychology and the public space: press services, speechwriters, communication protocols. In the Belarusian opposition in exile, these buffers are weak. The personal trauma of the leader's husband becomes a political event without passing through an institutional filter.

**Risks to political capital:**

- **Reputational.** The husband's behavior creates an associative chain with the traits the opposition criticizes in Lukashenko.
- **Team.** Conflict with the office undermines the team's authority and creates an impression of split leadership.
- **Gender.** Public subordination of the wife to the husband is perceived as a regression on gender policy.
- **Narrative.** If the opposition's message is "we are against authoritarian culture," and the leader's closest ally demonstrates this culture in micro-scale, the message is discredited by its own bearer.

**Fact.** Gender researcher Irina Sidorskaya notes that the personal life of politicians becomes a public matter when their behavior "affects the team, touches resources, trust, reputation of the movement."

## 5. August 2026: the @gypsynkov episode and escalation in the media space

August 2026 became a point of clustering of conflict behavior. When major media began to ignore Tsikhanouski's outbursts, he switched to more accessible targets - individual bloggers and journalists on social media. This changes the nature of the conflict: from criticism of institutions to personalized harassment.

In this wave, an episode occurred with blogger **@gypsynkov** (Mikhail Tsyhankou). Details in open sources are fragmentary, but structurally it fits the pattern: toxic communication with a media representative as a way to restore a sense of control. This is similar to the Lukashist practice of harassing journalists, but without access to state repressive resources.

**Limitation.** The specific content of the altercation with @gypsynkov is fragmentary. The inclusion of this episode is based on the client's request and the general context of the August escalation.

## 6. Scenarios of institutional response

**Prescription.** Everything that follows goes beyond descriptive analysis ("what is") and moves into the normative plane ("what to do"). This block is explicitly marked as prescriptive.

### 6.1. Separation of public roles

Clear delineation of zones of responsibility. The political Office must have an autonomous communication protocol that does not allow interference from the "first family" based on personal emotions. Advantages: eliminates associative transfer, protects the team, creates a vertical of responsibility. Costs: can be interpreted as a "family rupture"; requires political will; may provoke escalation from Tsikhanouski.

### 6.2. Media code

Development of a regulation for interaction with journalists and bloggers. Transition from "reactive aggression" to "proactive communication." Ban on joint improvised streams, mandatory coordination of appearances with the press service, buffer zone between personal social media and the official position. Works only with voluntary consent of both sides.

### 6.3. Psychological support as a political task

Recognition that working with post-traumatic states of key figures is not a "private family matter" but a task to ensure the stability of the political project. Advantages: turns vulnerability into a resource. Costs: requires Tsikhanouski's recognition of the need for help; in post-Soviet political culture, therapy is perceived as weakness.

### 6.4. Transformation of the "first gentleman" role

Transfer of Siarhei Tsikhanouski's role from the status of "undefined participant" to the status of "symbolic figure" with clearly defined (and non-overlapping with the Office) tasks, to eliminate the struggle for influence within one structure.

| Scenario | Short-term risk | Long-term risk | Probability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role separation | Media scandal | Reputation restoration | Low |
| Media code | Resistance, leaks | Communication stabilization | Medium |
| Psychological support | Risk of reading as weakness | Turning trauma into a resource | Low |
| Status quo | Periodic crises | Brand erosion, demoralization | High |

**Interpretation.** The status quo is not a neutral choice. It is an active scenario that cumulatively accumulates reputational damage. The probability of its implementation is high not because it is optimal, but because it requires no decisions. If the dynamics continue (new conflicts every 2-3 months), by mid-2027 the project's reputational capital may decline to a critical level.

## 7. Grounds and limitations

### Grounds

- Repeated public acts of aggression are documented by independent sources (Meduza, Nasha Niva, Belsat, DW, BBC).
- Testimonies of former allies (Bespalov, Tokarchuk) correlate in their description of behavioral style.
- Parallels with Lukashenko are expressed not only by external observers but also by Tsikhanouski's close circle.
- August 2026 demonstrates clustering of conflict behavior, which strengthens the status of a pattern over a single incident.

### Limitations

- Absence of clinical data; psychodynamics is interpretation, not diagnosis.
- Possibility of a conscious "bad guy" strategy to attract attention.
- Fragmentary data on the @gypsynkov episode.
- Conflict of interest in some sources (Bespalov is a former employee).
- **Confirmation bias.** The bulk of the data is obtained from critics and former allies. Materials from supporters or neutral observers have not been found in open access. This does not make the facts invalid but creates a risk of source homogeneity.
- The effect of prison trauma can explain behavior but does not justify it; the distinction between explanation and justification must be maintained.

### Meta-limitation of the normative block

All scenarios in Section 6 are based on external observation and do not account for the internal dynamics of the Tsikhanouski family, their informal agreements, and the actual distribution of influence. This block is not an instruction but a map of possibilities.

## 8. Conclusions

The public behavior of Siarhei Tsikhanouski after his release demonstrates a stable pattern structurally similar to Alexander Lukashenko's authoritarian communication style: personalization of conflict, rejection of criticism, rudeness as an instrument, attempts to control the media, and a tendency to subordinate family relations to political hierarchy.

The difference in scale and resources does not cancel the symbolic risk: an opposition leader whose closest ally (and husband) reproduces traits associated with the regime undermines his own narrative. For Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya, this creates a dilemma between personal loyalty and political responsibility.

August 2026, including the @gypsynkov episode, fits into the overall picture as an escalation of toxic communication. If the trend continues, the main damage will be done not to the Lukashenko regime but to the reputation of Belarus's democratic movement as an alternative to authoritarian culture.

> "No politician has ever won a war with the media." - Alexander Klaskouski, political analyst.
